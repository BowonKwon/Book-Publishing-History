Chapter 1. From manuscript to print (500–1500)

Key point. Estimated manuscript production in Western Europe rises from about 14,000 in the sixth century to about 5 million in the fifteenth. These figures are worked back from the manuscripts that survive, and the authors themselves describe them as low estimates.

Estimating what was made from what survives

No record says how many manuscripts were copied in a given medieval year. In a 2009 article in the Journal of Economic History, Eltjo Buringh and Jan Luiten van Zanden estimated production by century from surviving manuscripts, correcting for the uneven survival of material from different regions and periods. The chart below uses their results as reproduced in a table by Our World in Data, which notes that the authors ask for the figures to "be interpreted as low estimates."

The estimates cover eleven Western European regions (Italy, France, Germany, Iberia, the British Isles, the Netherlands, Belgium, Austria, Bohemia, Switzerland, Central Europe). In the sixth century Italy accounts for 10,194 of the regional total of 13,551. In the ninth century France and Germany lead; in the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries Italy is again the largest.

Around 1450

The roughly 5 million manuscripts of the fifteenth century include those copied after printing began. The same paper's estimate for printed books, in the Our World in Data table, is 12,587,000 for 1450–1500 (Chapter 2). The unit of that number is unclear. Our World in Data labels it only "books," while its notes say the unit of analysis for printed books is a "(new) 'title' or 'edition'." Yet the ISTC lists 28,530 editions for the same period, so 12,587,000 looks less like a count of editions than like editions multiplied by copies per edition (the ratio is about 441). Until the unit can be checked against the paper's own tables, this site calls the series "printed copies (estimate)" and does not set it directly against the manuscript estimate.

Sources for this chapter

  • Buringh, Eltjo, and Jan Luiten van Zanden (2009), "Charting the 'Rise of the West': Manuscripts and Printed Books in Europe, A Long-Term Perspective from the Sixth through Eighteenth Centuries," Journal of Economic History 69(2): 409–445.
  • Our World in Data, "Manuscript production per century" table (downloaded November 2026). In this table France alone is stored at years 50 past the start of the century (550, 650, and so on); this site groups those values with the same century.
  • The details of the estimation method and the unit of the printed-book values have not been checked against the paper's tables. The description above relies on Our World in Data's note: "The authors corrected the numbers to account for the underrepresentation of estimates for different geographical regions and different times."

Chapter 5. The age of national statistics (1945–1999)

Key point. Korea, Japan and China all kept official publishing statistics from the end of the war or liberation. Their lines differ in source and in definition. The Korean Publishers Association series falls from 41,712 titles in 1990 to 22,769 in 1991, and a footnote says only first and revised editions were counted from that year.

Three countries

Country Source What is counted Period on this site
Korea Korean Publishers Association, "Publishing totals 1946–2025" Books deposited with the association; "not statistics for the publishing industry as a whole" (footnote) 1946–2025
Japan Statistics Bureau, Historical Statistics of Japan, table 26-1 (from Shuppan Nenkan) New titles 1945–2005
China National Bureau of Statistics, China Statistical Yearbook 1999 table 20-75 and 2024 table 23-1 Book titles (new and reprinted), with new titles given separately 1952–2023 (selected years before 1998)

In Japan, new titles number 658 in 1945, 13,009 in 1950, 18,754 in 1970, and 40,576 in 1990. In China there are 13,692 titles in 1952 and 27,571 in 1957, then 4,889 in 1970, 14,987 in 1978, and 80,224 in 1990. The NBS tables give only two values between 1966 and 1976, for 1970 and 1975.

In the Korean series the 1950 cell is blank. The values for 1946–1948 (1,000, 950, 1,200) are round numbers ending in zero, and the association's table does not name their original source. The series reaches 1,618 in 1960, 2,633 in 1970, 20,985 in 1980, and 41,712 in 1990.

1991

The Korean series falls to 22,769 in 1991. The footnote reads, in translation, "from 1991 only first and revised editions are counted," which implies that reprints were included up to 1990. Fink-Jensen's per capita series (whose documentation says that country values for the 1930s to 1996 come from Plopenanu and others, 2014, based on UNESCO data) also shows Korea falling, from 917.4 per million in 1990 to 679.1 in 1991. The two series turn down in the same year, but by different amounts (45 percent and 26 percent). This site has not yet compared the underlying figures.

Translations

The Korean series reports translated titles from 1970. Their share of all titles is 18.8 percent in 1970, 10.3 percent in 1980, and 8.1 percent in 1990. It jumps to 17.1 percent in 1991, the year the definition of the denominator changed, and shifts again in 2005, the year comics were added. How much of each move comes from the change of definition cannot be separated in this table. The highest share is in 2003 (39.2 percent).

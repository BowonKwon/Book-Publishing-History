Chapter 0. How do you count "one book"?

Key point. When two sources give different book counts for the same country and year, the cases checked for this site mostly turned out to count different units or different scopes. Every chart on this site draws one line per source and marks the years in which a source says it changed its definition.

What counts as one

Statistics on books use at least four units: the edition (a new setting or printing of a work), the record in a bibliographic catalog, the title as defined by a statistical agency, and the copies printed. Union catalogs of early modern Europe list surviving books edition by edition; national statistics count the titles that appeared in a given year. The two cannot be joined into one line.

The UNESCO General Conference adopted a recommendation on the international standardization of book production statistics on November 19, 1964. It defined a book as "a non-periodical printed publication of at least 49 pages, exclusive of the cover pages," and a pamphlet as one of 5 to 48 pages (para. 6). Statistics were to report the number of titles and, if possible, copies (para. 7). A reprint is "unchanged in contents and layout, apart from correction of typographical errors," and a reprint by a publisher other than the original one counts as a re-edition (para. 6); reprints are counted in copies only, not as titles (para. 8). Excluded were advertising publications whose text is subsidiary and which are distributed free, publications of a transitory character such as timetables, price lists and calendars, music scores in which the music matters more than the words, and maps other than atlases (para. 2).

Same year, different numbers

Countries adopted the standard at different times and in different ways, and within one country the body doing the counting changed. These are the breaks found for this site.

Korea in 1991 shows the size of such a break. The Korean Publishers Association (KPA) series falls from 41,712 titles in 1990 to 22,769 in 1991. A footnote says that from that year only first and revised editions were counted. Reading the drop as a change in publishing would require separating out the part due to the new rule, and the table does not allow that. The rise to 43,598 in 2005 (from 27,527 in 2004) likewise coincides with the inclusion of comics.

An ISBN is not a title

The easiest number to find today is the count of ISBNs registered. A work issued in print, as an e-book, and in hardcover gets a separate ISBN for each. WIPO's report says this means "there will inevitably be some double counting." Japan registered 902,311 ISBNs in 2022, 717,326 more than the 184,985 of the year before; in the same year the Shuppan Shihyo Nenpo counted 66,885 new titles. Chapter 6 returns to this gap.

Rules this site follows

  • A different source gets a different line. Separate sources are not spliced into one "estimated" line.
  • Only definition changes that a source states itself get a vertical line. Breaks we merely suspect are mentioned in the text.
  • Counts of surviving books (early modern catalogs, old-book holdings) and counts of books published in a year (national statistics) are not drawn on the same axis.
  • No causal claims. The text goes no further than "around this time, such and such is observed."
